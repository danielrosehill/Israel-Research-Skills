# Verdict scales

Both scales use the markup of the Israeli price over the international price,
after converting the international price to shekels and making sure both are
VAT-inclusive. The scales come from the source plugin. They are judgement
calls, not statistics.

## Markup scale (the serious one)

| Israeli markup | Verdict |
|---|---|
| ≤ 5% | **GOOD DEAL** |
| 5–15% | **FAIR** — a typical Israeli import markup |
| 15–30% | **ABOVE AVERAGE** |
| 30–40% | **MILD MARKUP** |
| > 40% | **HEAVY MARKUP** — work out the landed import cost before deciding |

Why 18% VAT does not by itself make a price "expensive": international RRPs are
often quoted ex-tax (US) or with a different VAT (EU 19–25%, UK 20%). Compare
like with like and say what the reference includes.

## Freier scale (the playful one)

This scale is wider at the bottom. It treats Israeli VAT plus a normal importer
margin as the baseline, so up to 30% is "not a freier".

| Premium | Verdict |
|---|---|
| ≤ 30% | *Not a freier.* Normal Israeli tax and import premium. Buy it. |
| 30–70% | *Mild freier.* Worth checking alternatives, but not outrageous. |
| 70–150% | *Freier territory.* Consider waiting, importing, or an alternative. |
| > 150% | *Full freier.* Ask whoever is flying in from abroad to bring it in their suitcase. |

When the premium falls in the "not a freier" bucket, explain the VAT and
importer-margin baseline, so the user understands why 25% is not a rip-off.
